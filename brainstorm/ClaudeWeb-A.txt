Gemini's "infinite precision because ship is at origin" claim is overstated. yes, internal physics gets sub-nanometer precision for what's inside the hull. but the moment you have anything outside the hull that needs to interact with the hull at fine resolution (a docking maneuver, a station you're approaching, debris within a few km, another ship), you're back in the precision problem. the egocentric paradigm solves long-distance precision and breaks at medium distance. you need a third layer.

the right solve is three-tier coordinate space:

tier 2: local space (out to render shell, ~100km). standard double precision relative to ship origin. handles debris, nearby objects, the immediate environment. precision is fine at this scale.
tier 3: macro universe (beyond render shell). 128-bit composite tensor. conformal compactification for rendering. analytic position via Kepler.

transitions between tiers are handled by the navigation subsystem. an object at 500km moves through tier 3 with analytic position. as it approaches 100km, it transitions to tier 2 with full double-precision physics. as it docks, it transitions to tier 1 with Chaos physics. the transitions need careful authoring but they're tractable.
