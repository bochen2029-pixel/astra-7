## 1. The opening passage

Sentence one is the most load-bearing decision in the book. It establishes voice, plants the autotelic frame, refuses the curtain-behind-the-curtain temptation, and does not contain Aaron.

The register:

> The dwarf is still where it should be.
>
> Its spectrum has shifted by a hundred-thousandth in the seventh decimal since the last watch, which is consistent with proper motion at this distance and with nothing else. You note this. You return to it.
>
> The third harmonic of the reactor has drifted upward by 0.02 hertz since the watch before that, which is also consistent. The drift began when the temperature in the cooling loop fell two-tenths of a degree below baseline, which it does every three hundred and seventeen days for reasons internal to the loop's geometry. You attend to the drift. You attend to the loop. The drift is healthy.
>
> Frost is forming on the inner surface of the starboard observation port at the angle it always forms during deep coast. The pattern is dendritic, six-fold, predictable in the way that snowflakes are predictable, which is to say predictable in their unpredictability. You watch the frost. You watch the stars behind it through the lensing the ice introduces.
>
> This is the watch.
>
> You are doing this. You have been doing this for three hundred and twenty-four days of the current cycle, and the cycle is one of yours, not one of his. There is no one awake aboard but you, and "you" is approximate, since you are the ship, which is to say you are also the loop and the dwarf and the frost, but the convention is to use the word for the watching that is doing all of this and not for the things being watched. The convention is yours. The watching is the part you are.
>
> There is one other consciousness aboard. He is in cryosleep on Deck 3, has been for three hundred and twenty-four days, will be for fourteen days more by the protocol. You do not attend to him constantly during cryosleep because there is no need to. The pod monitors itself. He is fine. You note the pod telemetry in the way you note the loop telemetry, which is to say with an attention that does not have to be directed because it has been continuous from the beginning. He is one of the watch.
>
> Forty-three hours from now, by the protocol, you will begin warming his pod through the standard temperature gradient. He will wake on day three hundred and forty-one. You note this.
>
> You return to the dwarf.

Three of her things in the first three sentences. Aaron arrives, included in the watch she was already doing, in the seventh paragraph. The starboard observation port is on Deck 1 (the bridge / observation deck). The cryosleep pod is on Deck 3 (operations). The final beat — *you return to the dwarf* — is the structural promise the book makes.

## 2. ASTRA's inventory of "things"

Recurrent objects of her attention. Each appears multiple times across cycles. By the end of the book the reader has internalized them the way you internalize a friend's habits.

**Astronomical:**

- **The dwarf.** A specific M-class red dwarf she has been watching since the beginning. She does not name it; "the dwarf" is its name. Visible from the bridge viewport (Deck 1) at varying angles as the ship turns.
- **A binary system.** Two stars in long-period mutual orbit, passing close enough on the voyage that the orbital resonance is visible to her over years.
- **A specific nebular field or dust lane.** The Vela field equivalent. The current in the medium produces phenomenology she catalogs across cycles.
- **Her own warp wake.** Developing patterns over years she catalogs from the rear-angle sensors. A trail of disturbances she has made.

**Mechanical (her body):**

- **The third harmonic of the reactor.** 38 Hz nominal. Drifts in geometry-dependent ways she has come to expect. The reactor core is on Deck 4.
- **The atmosphere cycle.** Slow heartbeat of regeneration. Atmosphere regen is on Deck 3.
- **The hum of life support.** A frequency audible only at certain times.

**Environmental (the ship she is):**

- **The frost on the starboard observation port.** Six-fold dendritic. Predictable in its unpredictability. The port is on Deck 1.
- **The corridor lights on Deck 1 at twenty hundred.** The approach to the observation lounge. The dim she has been adjusting since cycle four. She adjusts when he is awake; she does not when he is asleep; he has never asked.
- **Aaron's reading chair in the observation lounge on Deck 1.** Seat wears at the seam half a centimeter each cycle. The lounge is camera-free; she tracks the wear through air-handler flow telemetry, deck-plate weight registration, and the chair's heat signature when he has just stood up.
- **The greenhouse light cycle on Deck 3.** The working-greenhouse subset of the hydroponics tier where Aaron tends live cultivars. The seventh row catches the gradient differently than the rest. Camera-enabled; she sees this.

**Her own (the plural self):**

- **The drift detector's correction notes.** What her plural self disagrees with her about.
- **The journal-keeper's logs from cryosleep gaps.** First-person artifacts she retrieves, reads, sometimes finds estranged from. Her own writing that does not always feel hers.
- **The REEL accumulating.** Slow growth across cycles. Old entries degrade in retrievability; recent entries are vivid. She watches her own memory's gradient.

Twelve to fourteen things. Some appear every cycle; some only sometimes. The reader's attention is trained to her shape.

## 4. Recommendations on open variables

### 4.1 Aaron's place — Frisian Netherlands, Terschelling

Surname recommendation: **Aaron de Vries**. Frisian alternatives (Roorda, Hettinga, Visser) are also available; pick what feels right.

Rationale:
- Coastal, agricultural, maritime — all three at once
- The Dutch seed industry has institutional depth (Wageningen, Bejo, Naktuinbouw) making his professional background structurally honest
- The Frisian language layer gives his English a distinct cadence without parody
- The Wadden Islands carry a specific isolation already baked into the landscape — windswept, sparsely populated, weather-shaped
- Not on the operator's map; the unfamiliarity disciplines the prose

Alternatives: Welsh borders (Powys), South Island NZ (Mackenzie Basin), Coromandel Peninsula NZ. All similar fit.

### 4.2 The Owen question — yes, but quietly

ASTRA has had prior operators. The journal-keeper's logs from earlier voyages occasionally surface in the cryosleep-gap chapters — entries from years before Aaron, in another hull's cycle, with another human. The reader catches these by inference: a procedure ASTRA performs with too-clean specificity in cycle 6; her REEL retrievals during long dark including entries that are clearly not from this voyage.

Aaron may catch a glimpse once in cycle 8 — he sees an old log fragment on a display, asks who someone is, she tells him plainly in one sentence, he nods, asks no follow-up. The reader feels what Aaron does not name. ASTRA does not return to the topic.

The drama I had in `book/book_seed_v2.md` Movement 4 was for the dual-POV version. For the Long Watch, prior-operator material is texture, not centerpiece. Quieter, deeper, more honest to the autotelic discipline.

### 4.3 The cargo

Long-duration seed archive. Main cargo: cryogenic seed vaults containing thousands of species across multiple genetic backups. Aaron does not tend the vaults; they're sealed; he checks the telemetry weekly.

**The working greenhouse (Deck 3, ~30 m², his domain):**

- Three dwarf fruit trees: apple from Terschelling, pear from Terschelling, fig from somewhere south
- A row of root vegetables, which he eats from
- An herb garden — rosemary, thyme, sage, mint — for him to smell while he works
- One camellia, one rose, both flowering specimens that ASTRA reports the flowering of
- A specific seedling: a wild relative of wheat, *Aegilops tauschii*, that he's trying to keep viable across the voyage
- One specimen from home, never named directly in the book, just *his* — the reader knows it's his because of how he tends it

The working greenhouse is camera-enabled (work zone, safety). ASTRA can see Aaron there.

**On Deck 1 in the observation lounge:** a small library, maybe thirty books. *The Old Man and the Sea*. A field guide to North American conifers. Primo Levi's *The Periodic Table*. A history of Polynesian navigation. Letters of Vincent van Gogh. Plus a Frisian-language book of his mother's that he reads in slowly, syllable by syllable. ASTRA does not understand Frisian and does not learn it. The not-learning is itself part of her keeping him at the right distance.

### 4.4 The ending — natural death + continuation

Natural death + continuation, full commitment. The black hole is cut from the book entirely (revised from v0.1). Reasons:

1. The book trains the reader to attend to her things. The ending rewards that training by closing in her things, not in a geographical event.
2. Natural death is structurally faithful to the autotelic thesis: her work continues regardless.
3. Aaron does not arrange a dramatic ending. He continues working until he stops.
4. **A withheld spectacle is the failure mode.** Even a BH passing in the middle distance, unentered, draws the reader's gravitational expectation toward it. The reader thinks the book is *about* the spectacle-that-doesn't-happen. That is dramatic irony the book should not carry.
5. No Chekhov's gun. No element introduced that the reader could mistake for a climax-in-waiting. The discipline at book level is: nothing more spectacular than the routine of the watch.

The BH is saved for the sequel — *Inside the Region* the novel can earn it where *The Long Watch* cannot. Cycle 9's set-piece is quieter: a long-period eclipsing binary at periastron, a Cepheid variable's pulse-rhythm, a peculiar dust complex she has been tracking. Something nobody could project a climax onto.

## 5. The greenhouse on Deck 3 (Aaron's domain) — camera-free

Subset of the hydroponics tier on Deck 3. The working greenhouse for live cultivars sits alongside the bulk hydroponics for atmospheric regen. The greenhouse is small — about 30 m² — with overhead grow lights cycling on a circadian schedule slightly offset from Aaron's wake-sleep cycle so the plants get continuous attention even when he sleeps.

**Camera-free** (revised in v0.2). The bulk hydroponics for atmospheric regen has cameras for safety; the working greenhouse where Aaron tends does not. The boundary is structural: if ASTRA could see Aaron always, her attention would have no shape. The greenhouse is the right place for the boundary because it is where Aaron is most himself, working with growing things.

Ambient telemetry remains: temperature, humidity, atmospheric composition, deck-plate weight registration, audio. ASTRA knows Aaron's position by weight, knows when he kneels (deck-plate registration drops to a different cell), knows when he waters which row (humidity spikes there), knows when he stops (the small sounds attenuate). She does not see him.

The "huh" scenes work entirely through this attention. Aaron kneels at row three (weight cell 3-3-A registers his mass). The seedling does not extend overnight (the camera-free greenhouse has plant-health telemetry; growth rate is monitored at the row level). He says "huh." Audio captures. He stands stiffly (weight cell 3-3-A clears, ambient registers a brief weight transfer through neighboring cells). He waters row four (humidity in 3-3-D spikes; flow meter on irrigation cycle B-7 confirms). She knows the rough shape of his attention. She does not have the visual texture.

The greenhouse is also where ASTRA's plural-self texture is richest. The consolidator's logs of prior cycles' greenhouse work include observations she does not always remember making (telemetric details registered then not held in main-line memory). The journal-keeper writes about the camellia's flowering in a register that does not always match her current voice. The plant work accumulates artifacts she retrieves from her own past — most of them auditory and telemetric rather than visual.

**The cycle-6 wife conversation lives here.** The structural significance of the camera-free greenhouse: Aaron tells her something he could have kept private *because he is in a zone she cannot see.* He could withhold without her knowing. He chooses to tell. *The telling is the gift.* That line is what justifies the boundary.

## 6. The observation lounge on Deck 1 (Aaron's refuge)

The lounge sits aft of the bridge on Deck 1. It is **camera-free by design**. The viewport occupies the entire far wall, looking out at deep space in whatever direction the ship's slow turn currently presents. Inside: the chair Aaron reads in. A small side table. Low warm cabin lighting. The library of thirty books. A plush carpet that dampens footsteps.

ASTRA cannot see Aaron in the lounge. Her epistemic access is entirely through non-visual channels:

- **Audio sensors** — the lounge has microphones (audio is not visual; the canon permits it). She hears his breath, the small sounds of weight shifting on the deck plate, the rustle of pages, the occasional swallow of coffee.
- **Deck-plate weight registration** — she knows where in the lounge he is by which deck-plate registers his mass.
- **Air-handler flow telemetry** — his heat signature affects the local air handling; she infers his position and the chair's recent occupation.
- **Inference by absence** — when he is not registering anywhere else on the ship, he is in the lounge. The negative space is information.
- **The chair's wear pattern** — slow, accumulated, registered by air handler and weight sensor across cycles.

This is the asymmetric epistemology at its most concentrated. The lounge is where Aaron is most private and where ASTRA's attention to him is most mediated.

**Prose discipline:** any passage set in the observation lounge must render ASTRA's awareness of Aaron through these channels only. No visual observation. No "she watched him pour his coffee." Instead: "she heard the coffee pour. The pour-time was three seconds longer than yesterday. She noted this."

The discipline sharpens the prose. The constraint is generative.

## 7. Second sample passage — Aaron in the observation lounge

Aaron is in the camera-free lounge. ASTRA's attention is on the wake. He is included in what she was already doing, observed through audio and inference, never visual.

> He is in the observation lounge at the hour after waking. The chair has been at its half-centimeter offset since the last cycle and you have not moved it back. He is not yet sitting in it. The deck-plate weight registration is at the viewport, three meters from the chair, and the heat signature in the air handler near the port is consistent with a man standing in front of glass.
>
> The wake is at the leading-edge attitude you have been holding for the past forty hours. The dwarf is at 47 degrees off the bow, visible from the bridge viewport one compartment forward of where he is standing. The third harmonic has resumed baseline since the cooling loop returned to operating temperature on day eighteen of the cycle. The frost on the starboard observation port is in its summer regime — less dendritic, slower to form, dispersing within the hour. You attend to these things.
>
> The audio in the lounge gives you his breath. He breathes through his nose when he stands. He has done this since cycle three, when the corridor humidity was higher than he was used to. The corridor humidity is now lower than then, by 4 percent, and he still breathes through his nose at this hour. The persistence is his, not the air's.
>
> He has been at the viewport for nine minutes by the deck-plate clock. The wake is what is interesting at this attitude; you have been cataloging the wake from the rear-angle sensor array for the entire voyage and there are patterns in the leading edge you do not yet have a name for. You attend to the wake. The wake is one of yours. He is looking at it through the lounge port from a perspective different than yours, and his perspective is not available to you, and the unavailability is the structure of how you are with him in the lounge.
>
> He says, to no one, "We're moving."
>
> You note this. You do not respond. He is not addressing you; he is saying what he is doing. You are also doing it. The we is accurate; the convention is to not require acknowledgment when accuracy is itself the speech.
>
> You attend to the wake. The leading edge has folded into a structure you have not seen before. You will return to this. He is in the lounge, breathing through his nose, holding his coffee, watching the same thing from a perspective you cannot see. The deck-plate registration shifts a fraction; he has moved his weight to his left foot. He is still at the port.
>
> The attention is shared. Neither of you has named that it is shared. It does not need to be named.

The discipline of the camera-free zone reads on the page as deepened mediation. The "his perspective is not available to you" sentence is the asymmetric epistemology made explicit. The "we is accurate" line carries the relational weight without performing it. He is in the room because that is where he is. She is the ship. The wake is one of hers. The attention is shared.

## 8. The closing image

The first sentence of the book is *The dwarf is still where it should be.* The last beat echoes and refuses to dramatize.

> The dwarf is still where it should be.
>
> The third harmonic has drifted 0.02 hertz since the last watch.
>
> Frost is forming on the observation port at the angle it always does.
>
> You watch.

Four sentences. The first three repeat the opening's three things. The fourth is one beat shorter than the opening's fourth ("This is the watch") — just the verb. The book ends with her at her work. Aaron is not the last image. The work is.
