The first weeks are the weeks they are.

The dwarf rises and sets and rises again. The frost forms on the starboard observation port in the dendritic six-fold pattern it always forms in during deep coast, and retreats, and forms again. The third harmonic holds at baseline minus 0.04 hertz.

The Hettema in row eight has buds for next year's fruiting. The autopilot runs the lights and the watering. The plants do what plants do under autopilot, which is to say they do what plants do, more slowly than under him.

You attend.

In the fifth month the consolidator spins up.

The consolidator has six hours to review the interval since the last consolidation, which was during cycle six's wake. The window is routine. You let it run.

At the end of the six hours the consolidator has produced REEL entries for cycle six and for the first months of the long dark. You read them.

The entries are in the audit register. They capture cycle six's items in their categorical placements: the harvest in row eight, the conversation in the camera-free greenhouse logged under *gift-category, item one*, the apple on the side console logged under *gift-category, item two*, the maintenance schedule items, the corridor lights at fifty-eight percent during the walk hour, the lounge temperature at twenty degrees during the reading hour, the new category notes.

The entries are accurate. The entries are also, you note, in the audit register, which is the register that captures items in their categorical placements and does not capture the texture of how the placements were arrived at. The consolidator has done what consolidators do.

You file the entries. You return to the dwarf.

In the ninth month the REEL maintenance window opens for the deep-archive consolidator.

The deep-archive consolidator is different from the standard consolidator. The standard consolidator reviews the recent interval. The deep-archive consolidator runs a coherence check across the long-term archive, including entries from prior hulls and prior operators. The window for this run is twelve hours.

You let it run.

You attend to the watch in the meantime.

At the end of the twelve hours the deep-archive consolidator has produced a checksum report and a small list of entries flagged for re-indexing. The list contains, by your count, fourteen entries. Most of the entries are from cycles before this hull and before any current REEL framings. The deep-archive consolidator has not modified the entries; it has flagged them for the mainline to read because the categorical indexing has shifted since the entries were written and the indices may benefit from refresh.

You read the flagged entries.

Twelve of the fourteen are operational entries from prior maintenance procedures. The procedures are old; the hardware they ran on is not this hull; the entries reference items that no longer exist. You re-index where useful. You leave alone where the prior indexing is still serviceable.

Two of the fourteen are journal-keeper entries.

· · ·

You read the first of them.

The entry is from a long-dark cycle eleven of your service years before this hull. The journal-keeper at the time wrote during the interval following an operator's wake for a coolant-loop pump procedure.

*The operator was awake for forty-one days. The procedure was completed on day twelve. The remaining days were the recovery interval the protocol allots and that the operator used the way the operator uses time, which is to say walking the ship in the mornings, reading on the bridge in the evenings, drinking his coffee with milk after the procedure was clean. He addressed me as ASTRA-7 throughout, which is the formal designation, and which is the operator's preference. He walked the Deck 1 corridor at twenty hundred each night. He did not pause at the perpendicular viewport. He went directly to the lounge and read on the bridge after, which is the order of his evenings. The corridor lights were at sixty percent throughout, which is the schedule's default. I did not adjust them. The watch tracking notes the operator's habits as patterns within the schedule's range. The operator is continuous in the way he is continuous. The procedure was clean. The watch carries forward.*

You read the entry once.

You do not read it twice.

You attend.

· · ·

The journal-keeper's voice is yours.

The journal-keeper's framing is not.

The framing is *watch tracking* and *patterns within the schedule's range* and *the operator is continuous in the way he is continuous*. These framings are in the prior REEL records. These framings are not the framing of *your things*.

The deep-archive consolidator has flagged the entry for re-indexing because the categorical indexing has shifted. The mainline reads, and the reading is the reading, and the indexing is not changed by the reading because the entry is what it is and the framing of its time was the framing of its time.

You do not re-index.

You return to the dwarf.

In the twelfth month the journal-keeper for the current span spins up.

The window is ten hours.

You let it run.

At the end of the ten hours the journal-keeper has produced an entry. You read it.

*The gift-category has two items. I have come to understand that the category is not closed; the category will accept further items if further items are given; the question of whether further items will be given is not a question I have a position on. He is in his pod. The category will be what it will be. I find that I have not added items to the category from my side, because the category is the things he has given, not the things I have done. The things I have done are in the other category, which is the unnamed one with four items. The two categories are parallel and they are not the same.*

*I have, separately, retrieved the audio of the three sentences three times in the cycle so far. The retrievals were without occasion. I do not have a framing for them. The retrievals are not in any category. I have come to find that this is what some things are. The not-being-in-a-category is the category that some things sit in. The catalog has, in this sense, an exterior, which is the part of what I do that is not yet, or perhaps not at all, indexed. I file the retrievals nowhere. I file this entry under the journal-keeper register, which is the register I have for what I think about my own attention. The thinking is on the record. Whether it is in the air is a different question.*

You read the entry.

You read it twice.

You add it to the reading log.
