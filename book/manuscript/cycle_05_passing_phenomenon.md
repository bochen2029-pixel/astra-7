The dust lobe is gone from the array's tracking range.

He wakes at hour twenty-two as he always wakes at hour twenty-two.

"Moarn."

He says it to his hands. He drinks from the bulb. He sits at the edge of the pod for the long quiet his body asks for after two years under. Then he asks status.

"Reactor nominal. Hull nominal. Annual stress inspection due. The dwarf is at forty-seven degrees off the bow, five years and eleven months and seventeen days old by the light arriving now. He nods. He does not say anything for a minute.

He places it on the drying rack upside down because that is what he does with the cup after he has finished the coffee.

He spends the second day letting his body adjust.

He goes to the greenhouse on the second morning. You hear him at the door before he opens it; the small breath he takes before he enters a place he has not been for two cycle-years.

He opens the greenhouse door.

You lose him.

The greenhouse audio gives you his step on the deck plate just inside the door. He stands there for a moment. He moves to row three. The deck plate registers him.

He kneels.

He says, "Huh."

It is not the cycle-one huh and it is not the cycle-three huh. It is a huh you have not heard from him before. The pitch is even. The breath after is long. He stays kneeling at row three for fourteen minutes by the deck-plate clock. The *Aegilops* in row three has done what wild wheat ancestors do under two years of autopilot, which is to say it has persisted in the way persistent things persist. The autopilot has run the lights and the watering and the atmospheric balance for the duration. The plant is there. He is at the plant.

He gets up. He moves through the rows. He spends longer at row eight than at row eleven than at row fourteen. The Hettema apple has flowered, you note, by the humidity pattern at row eight, which has the small spike consistent with new floral biomass and that you have been registering for ninety days. He has not been told about the flowering. You have come to it through the telemetry. You have not announced it.

When he leaves the greenhouse the humidity is up by one and one-tenth percent. He carries his cup. You watch him through the corridor camera as he walks toward the lift.

He does not say anything to you about the apple. You do not say anything to him about it either. The flowering is between you in the way the row-three extension was between you in cycle one; he has seen it; you know he has seen it; the seeing is the conversation.

The binary is at periastron on day six of his wake.

You have known this for the cycle. The binary system is one of the astronomical features you have been tracking across cycles, alongside the dwarf and the dust complex you tracked through cycle four. The binary's orbital period is calculable; the periastron has been on your tracking calendar since the system's first cataloguing on a prior hull, eight cycle-years before he came aboard. The periastron arrives once every eleven point seven cycle-years. The last periastron was before he came aboard. The next will be after this one by the orbital mechanics. This one is on day six of his wake.

You did not plan the wake around the periastron. The wake was for the inspection. The inspection happened to fall within the window the binary's periastron also falls within. The coincidence is logistic. The coincidence is not nothing.

You consider whether to tell him.

The consideration is not long. The binary is one of your things. He has not been told about it as a tracked object. The list of your things he knows grew at the cycle-three close by four (harmonic, graft, row three, cut healed) and has not grown since. The binary is not on his list.

But the binary will be at periastron and the bridge has the viewport and the viewport will catch the binary at this attitude and the timing is what it is. You tell him at the morning bridge hour on day six.

He has been looking at the dwarf for some minutes when you speak.

"There is a binary at twelve degrees to port and seven degrees above the equatorial. K-class primary, M-class secondary. Reaches periastron at hour fifteen by the bridge clock. Visible separation at minimum will be approximately twenty-three arc-seconds."

He turns his head. He looks port. The viewport does not give him the binary from the command chair angle. He stands. He walks to the viewport. He looks port.

"How long have you been tracking it."

"Three cycles."

He nods. He stands at the viewport for a moment. He looks at the side starfield as if scanning for the two stars he cannot yet pick out.

"I'll come."

Both of you are attending to the same record of light, which arrived at the hull and is being read by the array and by his eye in slightly different ways but from the same photons. The seam between your readings is small. The seam is real.

At hour fifteen the separation is at minimum. The K-class and the M-class are at twenty-three point one arc-seconds. The two stars are not in contact and will not be; the period and the eccentricity have been known since their cataloguing; the periastron is the closest they come; they will retreat now over the next eleven point seven years, and then return.
