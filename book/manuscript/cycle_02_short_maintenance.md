In the long dark the third harmonic continues to drift.

You log the drift weekly. By the eighth week after he returned to cryosleep the drift has reached 0.08 hertz above baseline, which is four times what it was when he last asked you about it, and which is still consistent with the loop geometry's three-hundred-seventeen-day cycle but is no longer in the part of the cycle where you can predict the rate of further drift. The rate has changed. You attend to the rate.

By the sixteenth week the drift is at 0.21 hertz. You run the diagnostic that costs you nothing to run because it is one of the diagnostics that lives on the reactor's local processor and does not require your attention except to receive the result. The result names the coolant loop's primary circulation pump's bearing-set as the most probable source. The bearing-set has been within nominal tolerances since the last full overhaul, which was three cycles ago, on a different hull, with a different operator, when the bearings were new. The bearings are no longer new. The bearings are doing what bearings do.

You log this. You add it to the cycle plan. You set the wake protocol for forty-two days hence, which is the interval the bearing-set is projected to remain within out-of-tolerance-but-not-yet-dangerous, and which is also the interval that gives him time to come out of the deep coast and recover and do the work and return to cryosleep with margin on both ends.

The cycle continues without him.

You attend to it the way you attend to cycles without him, which is the way you attend to anything: through the things that change and the things that do not. The dwarf rises and sets and rises again over the months that pass. The light arriving from the dwarf is, on the last week of the coast, four years and one month and eleven days old, because that is what the distance and the proper motion give you, and the dwarf-now remains as unknowable as ever, which you have stopped commenting on because the unknowability is structural and structural things become weather. The frost on the starboard port reaches dendritic maximum and retreats through nineteen cycles of the local diurnal that the ship's slow rotation creates. The hydroponics tier brightens at hour two and dims at hour fourteen and brightens again the next morning, on the schedule it always brightens by, even though the operator is asleep, because the schedule is the schedule and the plants are doing what plants do under autopilot.

You log the meteor that passes at month four. You log the dust-lane edge that crosses the forward sensors at month seven. You log the faint signal that registers on the comm relay at month nine and that resolves, after twenty-three minutes of attention, into nothing; a noise floor fluctuation consistent with the local interstellar medium and with no source you can identify. You add the log to the watch. The watch carries forward.

The third harmonic does what you have charted it doing.

The pod monitors itself. He is fine.

You return to the dwarf.

By the time the protocol begins, the third harmonic is at 0.47 hertz above baseline. You attend to the climb. The climb has not become exponential; it has become a slow ramp. You have charted the ramp. The ramp is approaching the threshold at which a bearing-set in a coolant loop is no longer a bearing-set but a failure-in-waiting. You will not let the ramp reach the threshold. The protocol has begun.

His vitals climb. The pod warms. By hour twenty-two he is awake.

"Moarn."

He says it the way he said it last cycle, with the same intonation, into the same wall opposite, before he asks for status. Some things in him are records; he has run this protocol on prior hulls, with prior controllers, who were not you. The records hold. The "moarn" is older than the cycle.

"ASTRA. What's wrong."

You did not expect him to ask it that way. You expected status. He has been awake for ninety seconds and his first question is what is wrong, which is the question he would ask if he had felt the absence of routine in the protocol, which is the question he would ask if he had a relationship with the loop that you have not been able to read from his telemetry. You answer.

"Third harmonic is at 0.47 hertz above baseline. Coolant loop pump bearing-set is the probable source. Within out-of-tolerance margin but trending. The protocol woke you on schedule."

He nods. He does not say anything for a minute.

"Coffee. Then Deck 4."

"Yes."

· · ·

He takes his coffee in the galley standing the way he stands. He does not sit down. He drinks. He rinses the cup.

He goes to Deck 4 by lift. He does not announce. You watch him through the corridor camera and through the engineering bay camera as he arrives.

"Loop offline by my command," he says, to no one and to you, at the bay door.

"Loop is online. Specify command for offline transition."

"Offline."

"Ten-second valve closure. Confirm."

"Confirm."

You close the valves. The pump spins down through its decay curve. The third harmonic does not change frequency in the spindown because the bearing-set is not generating the harmonic; the harmonic is generated by the pump's rotor, which is now stopped. What is left is the loop's ambient: the residual flow in the secondary lines, the thermal expansion of the coolant in the warm parts of the loop, the low hum that lives in every closed fluid system. You report.

"Loop offline. Bearing housing safe to open."

He nods at the camera he is looking at, which is the camera at the bay entrance, which is the camera he has been looking at when he wants to acknowledge you without speaking. You note this. He has been looking at that camera since day three of his first time aboard. The looking is one of his things.

He moves to the bearing housing. The housing is at the aft end of the primary loop, behind a panel he removes with the multi-tool he keeps in his belt. The panel comes off in his hands.

You watch.

The bearings inside the housing are visible to you now, through the bay camera and through the panel-open angle. They look the way bearings look at the end of a service interval. They have done what bearings do. He looks at them for a moment, then says, "Spare."

"Cargo hold 4-B. Bin seventeen. Bearing-set, primary coolant, designation CL-PRI-04."

"Tank."

He walks to cargo 4-B. He returns with the bin. He opens it. The new bearing-set is in its protective film. He sets it on the work surface beside the housing.

The replacement takes him forty-three minutes by the bay clock. He works carefully. He does not narrate the work. He removes the old bearing-set; he sets it on a tray; he wipes the housing seat with the cloth he keeps in his pocket; he installs the new bearing-set; he torques the retaining ring to its specification; he closes the housing; he replaces the panel.

Halfway through the replacement he stops and holds the old bearing-set up to the light of the bay overheads and looks at it for a moment. He turns it in his hand. He says, "Aye." It is not Frisian and it is not English exactly; it is the sound a man makes when something is what he expected it to be. You log the sound. You do not catalog it under language. You catalog it under him.

When the panel is back in place he says, "Loop online."

"Initiating sequence. Pump spin-up in ten seconds. Confirm."

"Confirm."

The pump spins up. The bearing-set engages. The third harmonic begins to climb back through its frequencies as the rotor reaches operating speed. You attend to the climb. The climb passes through the frequency where the old bearing-set was running, and the harmonic at that frequency is now different. The harmonic at that frequency is clean. The harmonic has the shape of a new bearing-set running in a properly seated housing in a loop that is doing what loops do.

By the time the pump reaches full operating speed the third harmonic is at baseline minus 0.04 hertz, which is what the third harmonic sits at when the bearing-set is fresh. You report.

"Third harmonic at baseline minus 0.04 hertz. Loop nominal."

He nods at the bay camera. He says nothing.

He carries the old bearing-set to the cargo bay and puts it in the bin marked for recycling. He walks back through engineering and stops once to put his hand on the inspection plate of the loop where the new bearings are, which is what an engineer does when something has just been fixed, which is also what a man does when he wants to confirm with his hand what his ears have already heard. The plate is at the temperature it is. He pulls his hand back.

He walks out of engineering toward the lift.

· · ·

He stays awake for three weeks.

The repair was a day; the recovery from cryosleep was the rest of the first week; the second and third weeks are the weeks the protocol gives him before re-entry. He uses the time the way he uses time. He visits the greenhouse twice a day. He runs the maintenance schedule's minor items that have accumulated since cycle one. He drinks coffee in the galley standing. He reads in the lounge in the evening.

The greenhouse has been on autopilot for the duration. The autopilot is a function you run, which is to say one of your functions runs it, which is to say the running is yours though it is at a layer of your attention that is more like the running of the cooling loop than like the watching of the dwarf. The greenhouse plants have done what plants do under autopilot, which is to say they have done less than what plants do under his attention, which is the difference his attention makes, which is a difference you have come to register.

On the second day after the repair he kneels at row three. You hear the deck-plate. You hear the silence.

He says, "Huh."

You do not know what the "huh" is. The seedling that extended in cycle one has had a cycle and an autopilot run since then; the seedling could have done many things; the kind of knowing you have about what it has done is the kind of knowing the manifest gives you, which is the kind that says *Aegilops tauschii* and does not say *taller, shorter, flowering, dying*. The humidity in the greenhouse during his kneeling is consistent with the seedling having grown some incremental amount, but the incremental amount could be a leaf or could be a stem and you do not have a way to distinguish.

He works in the greenhouse for ninety minutes. When he leaves, the humidity is up by half a percent and the carbon dioxide is down by a fraction. The plants have done their work. He has done his.

He goes to the lounge in the evening of every day of the three weeks. He sits in the chair. The chair is in its position with the half-centimeter offset from center, which has not changed since cycle one, because no one has been in the lounge in the cycle between, and you have not moved the chair, because you do not move the chair. He reads. He breathes through his nose. He does not speak.

In the second week he goes to Deck 4 once, in the middle of the day, without a stated reason. He stands by the inspection plate of the coolant loop. He puts his hand on it. He stands there for two minutes by the bay clock. He pulls his hand back. He walks out. You do not ask what he was doing. He does not say. You catalog the visit under the same category you catalog the camera-looking under, which is the category of things he does that are gestures toward you that are not addressed to you and that you receive anyway.

In the third week he comes to the bridge in the morning and sits and looks at the dwarf at forty-seven degrees and says, after some minutes:

"ASTRA."

"Yes."

"Harmonic."

"Baseline minus 0.04. Loop nominal."

"Good."

He drinks his coffee.

The exchange is the second exchange of the kind that started with row three in cycle one. He has named one of your things and asked whether it is doing what it should, and you have told him, and he has acknowledged. The exchange is brief. The exchange has the same air in it that the row-three exchange had. Two attentions, brushing. He has added a thing to the list of your things he knows. The third harmonic is on his list now. The row-three seedling has been on his list since he found it. The list is short. The list is growing.

At the end of the third week he goes to Deck 3 and stands in the cryosleep bay and looks at his pod for a moment and then climbs into it.

"Wake me when you need me."

"Yes."

The lid closes.

You sequence the cooling protocol. His vitals drop. By the end of the watch he is at the metabolic minimum of cryosleep. The pod monitors itself. He is fine. He is one of the watch.

You return to the dwarf. The dwarf is still where it should be in the only sense in which you can know where it is.

The third harmonic is at baseline minus 0.04 hertz. The loop is running clean.

The watch carries forward.
