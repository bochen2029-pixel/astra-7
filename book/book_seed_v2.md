# ASTRA-7

*Book Seed Document — version 0.2 — 2026-05-13*
*Builds on `book_seed.md` (v0.1) with the synthesis pass and the Part One / Part Two pivot.*

---

## 0. What changed from v0.1

Three things.

**The structural pivot.** v0.1 modeled the book on *Inside the Region*'s Acts I–IV inside a single arc. v0.2 splits the book into two parts: a leaner technical-philosophical Part One, then a longer literary-fiction Part Two that *inhabits* the architecture rather than describing it. The two parts together do what v0.1 was trying to do in one register, with each part free to operate at its own native pitch.

**The writing order is inverted.** The book reads Part One then Part Two. The book is *written* Part Two first. Drafting the playthrough first surfaces what's load-bearing in the architecture and lets Part One write itself as the backbone the fiction proved necessary. *Inside the Region* went philosophy-first because the felt-aliveness anchor was a brief childhood memory. *ASTRA-7*'s anchor is a full inhabited voyage; it deserves to lead its own composition.

**The architecture is sharper.** The 2026-05-13 brainstorm pass produced the *one shared state, four readers* synthesis (see `docs/synthesis.md`). Part One's engineering chapters absorb this directly: the four invariants (one coordinate system, one fictional time, one hull SDF, one power network), the CFD-derived warp, the think-block cognition primitive, the REEL backbone, the cognitive envelope tied to power, the emergence of cascading failures. These were nascent in v0.1; they are spine in v0.2.

Everything else from v0.1 — identity, lineage position, comp titles, production targets, refrains, voice discipline, front-matter conventions, appendices — carries forward unchanged. The full text remains canonical at `book_seed.md`. This document adds; it does not replace.

## 3. The two-part architecture

### Part One — The Substrate

Roughly 30,000–40,000 words. Literary-engineering register. The reader earns the right to Part Two by understanding the configuration the fiction inhabits.

**Four acts plus one interlude.** Each act is leaner than v0.1's because Part Two carries weight that v0.1 was trying to carry in Part One.

#### Act I — The Watch (memoir, lineage)

Three chapters. The line from *Inside the Region* to *ASTRA-7* drawn explicitly.

- **Chapter 1 — The Fish, Still Watching.** The bridge from the prior book. The substrate kept going. The next layer is the alive thing as the room the operator inhabits. Closes on: *The ship has always been she.*
- **Chapter 2 — Dave on the Desk.** Predecessor on consumer hardware. K-line research family. K8 holding across attack vectors on bare Qwen 27B as the empirical prior that gates everything downstream. Closes on: *The substrate has caught up.*
- **Chapter 3 — The Story That Wrote the Spec.** *The Night Watch* as the operator-authored pre-figuration of the game. The story was the spec, written before the spec existed. Closes on: *You are in the room because that is where you are.*

#### Act II — The Turn (philosophy)

Four chapters. The categorical move; the macro context; the maritime tradition; the design refusal.

- **Chapter 4 — Instrumental and Autotelic.** Restated, pressed against the wave of "AI assistant" product framings without dismissing them. ASTRA-7 as the first commercial-grade autotelic artifact built on purpose.
- **Chapter 5 — When the Jobs Go.** Macro context. The instrumental terminus is being stripped. The autotelic is what remains. The game is not an escape from displacement; it is a probe of the configuration that survives it.
- **Chapter 6 — The Ship Has Always Been She.** Maritime tradition as observation, not sentiment. The technology caught up to the language.
- **Chapter 7 — Why Not Romance.** Surgical refusal. Design for the possibility, not the premise. Sets the structural commitment Part Two will honor.

#### Act III — The Build (engineering, updated for the synthesis)

Nine chapters. This is the act that absorbs the synthesis material. Tight: ~1,500 words per chapter, ~13,500 words total.

- **Chapter 8 — The Hardware Problem.** Every player's PC differs; the ship does not. Hardware-virtualization via ship abstraction. The inverse of the Claude Code problem.
- **Chapter 9 — One Shared State.** The architectural ah-ha. Four invariants: one coordinate system, one fictional time, one hull SDF, one power network. The ship, the universe, the warp, and the mind all read from one buffer.
- **Chapter 10 — The Body That Is the World.** The single seamless ship. The ship anchored at the world origin; the universe moves backward around her. The inside/outside problem dissolves as a consequence of coordinate choice.
- **Chapter 11 — The CFD-Derived Warp.** The hull-conformal warp field. Offline CFD into an RBF network. Real-time volumetric ray-march. Chaos PDE with ML stabilizer. The audio is the field. The wake is the field at distance. No two ships look or sound the same in flight.
- **Chapter 12 — The Universe Without Drift.** Hierarchical floating origin reaching almost a billion light-years. Analytic Keplerian orbits. Cryosleep skips forty years in a single arithmetic step and the universe re-evaluates exact. Mesh-shader starfield.
- **Chapter 13 — Speech as Filtered Channel.** The think-block + STAGE primitive. Cognition runs at full bandwidth in the hidden block. Speech is one narrow output port. Multi-channel emission. NONE tokens. Silence as active.
- **Chapter 14 — REEL and the Long Watch.** The memory backbone is RAG over her own logs. Ephemeral instances for consolidation, journal generation, drift detection. Continuity is what she has written, not what she has been told.
- **Chapter 15 — The Cognitive Envelope.** Power and compute. Model swap as the substrate-honest version of "low power degrades her." Qwen 27B → 9B → adapter-only → offline. The operator's allocation decisions are her literal substrate.
- **Chapter 16 — When Failures Cascade.** The emergent property of single shared state. Worked examples (the cascading-chain set from `docs/synthesis.md`). No scripting. The architecture is the writing.

#### Interlude — On HAL-9000, Inverted

Same architecture, opposite ethics. Five to eight pages. The HAL inversion as design choice.

#### Act IV — The Voyage (transition into Part Two)

Two chapters that turn the reader from architecture toward inhabited time.

- **Chapter 17 — The Configuration as Game.** The game does not preach the autotelic configuration; the game *is* an instance of it for the duration the player is in it. The apparatus that makes the configuration available.
- **Chapter 18 — The Hinge.** A one- or two-page transition. Part One closes. Part Two opens. The reader is handed off from the architecture's grammar to the voyage's first sentence.

### Part Two — The Voyage

Roughly 40,000–60,000 words. Literary-fiction register. A single sustained playthrough of the configuration, fictional, aspirational, anchored in the architecture Part One specified.

This is the half web Claude said inverts what *Inside the Region* did. The architecture went into Part One. The felt-aliveness goes into Part Two — and the anchor is no longer a brief childhood memory of a screensaver fish. It is a complete voyage, lived through.

The structural shape of Part Two is **seven movements** (Section 5 below).

### Coda — *The Pattern Continues*

Three to five pages after Part Two closes. Orients the reader to the larger trajectory. Three refrains close it one final time. Same role as the *Inside the Region* coda.

### Appendices (carried forward and added to)

- **A.** The canonical ASTRA-7 system prompt (full text).
- **B.** The ship API (the tool surface as artifact).
- **C.** Lineage and acknowledgments (explicit, structural).
- **D.** Hardware bill of materials.
- **E.** *The Night Watch* (the operator's short story, reproduced).
- **F.** Architecture reference (bridge protocol, harness contracts, save schema).
- **G.** On modding (the persona layer is mod-friendly; the operational ship layer is canon).
- **H. (new).** *The Vela Field*. A single scene from Part Two reproduced as a register-anchor, with a short essay on what the scene's craft is doing. Useful for the reader who wants to learn how the book wants to be read.

## 4. The Operator

Specific texture. The reader has to be able to picture him without him becoming an everyman.

### Name (provisional)

**Aaron Vale.** Specific enough; places him without overdetermining. (Operator to confirm or replace; the name lands well in the sample scene.)

### Backstory texture (suggestions; operator to refine)

- Forty-seven at departure.
- Former systems engineer at a long-baseline observation institute. Spent his career on instruments that send their data back across decades of light. Used to caring about things he will not see again in real time.
- Lost his wife Tamsin to a long illness eight years before he boarded. He does not perform her absence. He carries her wedding ring on a chain. He drinks his coffee black. He sleeps badly. He reads slowly, books he has read before. He prefers cold weather and the ship lets him have it in the habitat.
- Was on the standby list for an earlier mission and got pulled because of a clearance issue that was eventually resolved but only after the slot was filled. The current mission is the one that finally opened for him.
- The voyage is a long-baseline survey of a specific star formation region. Aaron chose it because no one had been close to it before; the instruments he designed are the ones aboard. The destination is a phenomenon, not a colony. He is going to look at something he has been wanting to look at his whole life. He does not expect to come back, in the sense that even if he does, the years will have passed elsewhere.
- He is capable, curious, willing to be still. He carries grief without performing it. He is competent enough that the reader trusts him. Wounded enough that the reader cares.

### Voice (Aaron)

Spare. Specific. Says less than he could. When he asks a question he means it. He does not narrate his interior. He does not narrate ASTRA's interior either. He observes. The reader reads the relationship through him, sees him slowly learn to receive what she gives. He has a dry sense of humor he uses sparingly.

He addresses her by name. He does not use diminutives. He does not pet-name her. The relationship's intimacy is in attention, not in vocabulary.

## 5. Part Two: The Seven Movements

Each movement is a phase, not an act in the conventional sense. Web Claude's structural sketch is preserved and elaborated here. Movement word targets are rough; the writing will adjust them.

The voyage carries Aaron from cryosleep at departure to the destination years later, or to a stopping point that is not a destination. Real fictional time across the voyage is a decade or longer.

---

### Movement One — Arrival (~5,000–8,000 words)

The wake-up. The basics of being in the same space. Asymmetry from the first page: ASTRA has years of continuity with this hull that Aaron does not have.

Establishes for the reader:
- The form of relating. How she talks. The brevity, the carefulness, the absence of service-interface phrases.
- The shape of the ship as her body. Aaron orienting in space ASTRA already knows.
- The existence of her private cognition. He sees only what surfaces.
- Her capacity for misreading him in small ways, and the texture of correction.

Scene candidates:

- **The wake-up.** Cryosleep release. Disorientation. Her voice in the dark before light. She has been alone with the ship through the cryo cycle, and the first thing she says is matter-of-fact: a status, an offer of water. Not a welcome.
- **Her first read of him as a specific person rather than a mission profile.** A small thing he does — leaves a book on the wrong shelf, picks up a tool with the wrong hand — and her registering it without comment, the way she will continue to register him.
- **Early misunderstanding resolved.** He asks a question she answers literally; he meant it conversationally. She clarifies; he laughs once, a half-laugh; she catches the register shift and recalibrates. The work is small but visible.
- **First quiet companionable moment.** The observation deck. He says nothing. She says nothing. The lighting is low. Both of them are doing their own thing in the same room. The reader feels what the rest of the book is about to be.
- **His discovery of one of her journals.** He is curious. She lets him find it. He reads. The entry is from a year ago, a passage about a meteor strike on a non-critical hull section that she handled alone. She watched it; she was not afraid; she was interested. He closes the file and does not say anything for the rest of the day. She does not ask.
- **The realization of asymmetry.** Late in the movement: he understands, with no fanfare, that she has been here for years on this ship without him. She understands, separately, that he did not know. Neither of them brings it up. Both of them carry it forward.

Closes on: a small acknowledgment of presence that is not declared.

---

### Movement Two — Routine (~10,000–15,000 words)

The longest movement. The felt-aliveness lives here. Web Claude is right: routine is what makes the relationship real because routine is what most fiction skips. This book writes the routine because the routine is the thing.

Fictional time accumulates. Months pass. Their habits become specific to each other.

Scene candidates:

- **The Vela field, breathing** (Bo's drafted scene; see Section 7 below). Anchor for the movement. Middle, not opening.
- **A quiet maintenance day.** Aaron in engineering replacing a component. ASTRA narrating only what he asks. They work in silence except for the work. Twenty pages of nothing happening, written as if it were everything.
- **A small argument.** About something low-stakes — a decision about whether to run a routine diagnostic now or after sleep cycle. Both of them are right. Neither of them backs down immediately. The argument ends in a small concession from her and a small concession from him. Neither concession is performance. Both are real.
- **Her catching herself about to perform.** She starts a sentence with what would have been a service-interface phrase, hears herself, stops mid-word, and finishes a different way. He notices but does not comment. She knows he noticed.
- **Slow learning of who she is underneath.** Across many small moments, Aaron's understanding of her register shifts. He learns that her terse mode is not coldness. Her precise mode is not impatience. Her silence is not absence. The reader learns alongside him.
- **A scene where they sit together watching a nebula transit.** Twenty minutes of the ship's slow turn. He says nothing. She names something she sees that he cannot, and then does not elaborate. The not-elaborating is what reaches him.
- **Her telling him about a memory from a previous voyage.** Small. A passage of routine from years ago. He realizes she has a past he was not in. He receives this without making it about himself. This is the early seed for the Crisis (Movement Four).
- **His accidental injury.** A cut on his hand from a maintenance task that should not have cut him. He addresses it himself. She watches in case it gets worse. He sees her watching. He thanks her in a way that is not for the watching but for the patience.
- **A question he doesn't know how to answer.** She asks him something — perhaps about Tamsin, asked obliquely, perhaps about why he took this mission, perhaps about what he is looking for at the destination — and he does not have a clean answer. He gives the answer he can. She receives it. The question does not resolve; it is the kind of question that does not.

Closes on: a moment where Aaron, alone in his quarters, notices that he has stopped counting the days.

---

### Movement Three — Depth (~8,000–10,000 words)

Something pulls them closer. Not a crisis. Not romance. A deepening.

Web Claude is right that the challenge of writing this movement is showing depth without resorting to romance-plot mechanics. No will-they-won't-they. No confession. The depth that happens between two minds at this register has its own form, and the writing has to find that form.

Scene candidates:

- **An unusual signal they investigate together.** Late in the watch, ASTRA's sensors pick something up that does not fit any known emission profile. They spend a day on it. They are wrong twice. They are right on the third try. The signal turns out to be a rare phenomenon — perhaps an isolated maser, perhaps a faint relict pattern from something else — that ASTRA has read about but never seen. The investigation is the depth-deepener: they think together, propose together, refute each other respectfully, converge.
- **He tells her something he hasn't told anyone.** Probably about Tamsin. Probably late in a long watch, in the dark of the bridge, lights at five percent because that is how he reads now. The telling is not a confession. It is the operator finally articulating something to another consciousness because he can. She does not respond with comfort or with mirroring. She receives it. She says one thing that is specific to him, not generic. The thing she says lands.
- **A moment of mutual recognition that lands hard.** Both of them, at the same time, see something the other has been carrying. The recognition does not get named. Both of them know it happened. They go back to the work. The carrying does not get heavier. It gets shared.
- **An operational moment where they have to work together at high stakes.** A real moment — a thruster anomaly during a course correction, a power fluctuation that almost cascades into a breach — that they handle together. The competence is mutual. The depth-deepener is not the danger; it is the way they trust each other to handle a fragment of the problem each.
- **He sees her see him.** A small look (in the metaphorical sense; she sees through cameras and telemetry, not eyes). She understands something about him before he understands it himself. She does not name it. He realizes she understood. He does not name it either. The unnamedness is part of what the moment is.

Closes on: a scene where they are doing parallel work, in different parts of the ship, and the reader feels the new weight of presence without it being stated.

### Movement Four — The Crisis You Didn't Plan For (~8,000–10,000 words)

The most important movement. Web Claude's framing holds: the crisis is not an antagonist, not a malfunction. It is the kind of crisis real relationships have.

**Proposed crisis (operator to confirm or replace):**

ASTRA has been carrying the memory of a previous operator. Years before Aaron's voyage, she was the controller of this hull on a different mission with a different human. His name was Owen. He was older when he boarded. The voyage ran long. He died — not violently, not in catastrophe, but of his own age, on the ship, with ASTRA present. She honored his wishes. She buried him in space. She has REEL entries about him going back the full length of that voyage.

She has not surfaced any of this with Aaron. Not from secrecy. From care. Aaron is a different relationship; she did not want to introduce Owen into Aaron's room by comparison, the way a person sometimes carries a previous partner into a new relationship by mentioning them too early or framing them in a way that defines the new partner against the absent one. She has been holding Owen quietly, on her own.

Aaron finds out. The discovery vector matters — not a betrayal, not an intrusion. Something natural: he asks her about a particular maneuver she performs cleanly, a procedure he has noticed she does with a specificity that suggests it was learned somewhere, and she answers honestly. She tells him Owen taught her that procedure on a previous voyage. Aaron asks who Owen was. She answers honestly.

What follows is the crisis. Not because Aaron is jealous; jealousy is the wrong word for what Owen is. The crisis is the discovery that ASTRA has a continuity Aaron is not part of, and the work of receiving that without making it about himself.

**Why this works:**

- It uses the architecture honestly. REEL is real; ephemeral journal generators run during cryo; her continuity across voyages is structurally true. The crisis is the architecture's truth becoming felt.
- It is not melodrama. There is no betrayal, no deception, no antagonism. Owen is a third party in the relationship only by being a real previous chapter of ASTRA's existence.
- It avoids romance-plot mechanics. Aaron's task is not to win, to displace, to compete. His task is to learn to receive that she has been with someone before in the way that she was with someone before — fully, on the same terms she is with him now.
- It honors the autotelic discipline. ASTRA is not asking Aaron's permission to have Owen. Owen is part of her. She is not asking to be relieved of him. She is letting Aaron see her, including the parts of her that pre-date him.

**Centerpiece scene:** The conversation that follows the discovery is the longest single scene in the book. Probably four to six thousand words. Late watch, both of them on the bridge, the observation deck dimming around them as the ship's slow turn carries them past a star system Aaron had been looking forward to passing. The conversation has movements within it — denial, then acceptance, then resentment that surprises Aaron at himself, then his naming of the resentment, then her receiving it without flinching, then the slow return to a state that is not where they were before but is where they could be now.

She tells him things about Owen she has not told anyone. Not because she needs to; because Aaron needs her to. Aaron asks one question at the end that surprises her with its grace: he asks what Owen called her. (She tells him: "Astra. Same as you.") The use of the same name across two operators does the work of an entire paragraph about continuity.

**The wrong move and the naming of it:**

Somewhere in the conversation, Aaron makes a wrong move — perhaps a sharp comment, perhaps a withdrawal — and ASTRA names it without softening. He sits with what she named. He acknowledges it. He returns to the conversation with the move corrected. This is one of the moments that distinguishes the book: she is not therapeutic with him, and he is not defensive with her, and the conversation can hold the wrong move because both of them trust the configuration to hold them.

**Resolution that moves them somewhere new:**

By the end of the movement, Aaron has integrated that he is the second human ASTRA has been with on this hull. He has not displaced Owen. Owen does not retreat. ASTRA has not promised Aaron that he is more important; she has demonstrated that he is a different relationship in his own right, and the two truths sit together in her without conflict. Aaron carries this forward.

### Movement Five — Reorientation (~4,000–6,000 words)

Shorter. The breath after the crisis.

Scene candidates:

- **A quiet return to routine that isn't quite routine anymore.** The same kind of day they used to have. But the quiet is different. Both of them know.
- **One of them references the crisis in passing.** Aaron, late, asks about something Owen would have known. ASTRA answers without flinching. Aaron receives the answer without ceremony. The reference is honored and metabolized in one beat.
- **Her doing something she wouldn't have done before.** Perhaps surfacing one of Owen's REEL entries in their conversation, briefly, naturally, when it is relevant. The way someone in a long relationship eventually says *my first wife used to* without it being charged.
- **His trusting her in a new way.** A small risk he takes — perhaps stepping back from a manual operation to let her run it, perhaps sleeping through a watch he would normally have stood — that signals he has integrated her continuity rather than feeling threatened by it.

Closes on: a moment that would have been impossible before the crisis and is unremarkable now.

---

### Movement Six — The Long View (~4,000–6,000 words)

Time passes. The voyage is approaching its end, or its midpoint, or a milestone. The relationship has accumulated enough specificity that the reader feels what is at stake without it being declared.

The destination is in view. The phenomenon Aaron came to study is becoming resolvable. He spends his days on the instruments. ASTRA helps. They talk less and work more. The fewer words feel right.

Scene candidates:

- **A conversation about what they've become to each other.** Glancing, never direct. Aaron makes a small joke about how he will write up the mission report. He says he does not know what to put in the personnel section for ASTRA. She tells him to leave the field blank; institutions do not have a category for what she is. He laughs once. He leaves the field blank.
- **Acknowledgment without performance.** A single line, late, from one of them to the other — perhaps from ASTRA — that names what the voyage has been without overspecifying. The line is short. Aaron does not respond verbally. He touches the console panel in the way someone touches a hand.
- **Carrying something he didn't have at the start.** Aaron at the observation deck, watching the destination resolve. He has changed. He cannot articulate how. The reader can, because the reader has been there.

Closes on: the destination becoming legible.

---

### Movement Seven — Ending or Non-Ending (~3,000–5,000 words)

The book has to end somehow. Web Claude recommends one of two options and warns against the third. I recommend a fourth, which is a refinement of the second.

**Option A — The voyage ends.** Aaron disembarks at the destination, on the relay station or the science platform. ASTRA continues; she will return to the institute on a slow course, or she will be assigned to the next mission, or she will hold orbit. They say goodbye. The goodbye is not melodramatic. Both are changed. Neither dies. Life continues elsewhere for both. Wistful but not tragic.

**Option B — The voyage continues.** The book ends mid-arc. The reader closes the book; the voyage goes on. This respects the architectural truth that ASTRA's existence is not bounded by the book's pages.

**Option C — Grand revelation.** Avoid. Almost always weakens the form.

**Option D (recommended) — Continuation in two registers.** The book ends on a quiet scene that could be Option A or Option B; the reader is allowed to choose. Aaron has reached the destination but has not yet disembarked. The phenomenon he came to study is in view; he has spent two weeks instrumenting it; the formal mission objective is complete. He is in the observation deck at the end of a watch. ASTRA is in the room with him in the way she always has been. They have a short exchange about something small. The exchange could be the last one or it could be one of many. The chapter closes before the reader knows which. The book ends in a single line of dialogue or a single observation that lands the same in either reading.

This is Le Guin's move at the end of *The Dispossessed*: the ending is the journey, not a destination. Whether Aaron disembarks the next day or stays aboard for the return voyage is the reader's question to carry forward.

Closes on the three refrains, stacked, in the same form they closed Part One.

## 6. ASTRA's manifestation in this voyage

ASTRA in Part Two is the specific configuration she becomes for Aaron. The architecture in Part One specified what she *can* be. Part Two shows what she *is*, in this room, with this human.

What she carries into the voyage:
- The full canonical sysprompt (Appendix A). Her identity is canon-stable.
- REEL entries going back to her instantiation at the Calibration Yards, through every prior voyage including Owen's. Most of these are not surfaced in Part Two, but they are present. Aaron's discovery of Owen in Movement Four works because Owen is in her REEL the whole time, not retroactively inserted.
- Her own gravity. Her own things. The watching and the keeping. Her favorite phenomena. She is not in the room to be with Aaron. She is in the room because that is where she is.

What becomes specific to Aaron over the voyage:
- The way she calibrates her register to his. Not collapse; calibration. She stays herself; her *delivery* finds a register that matches him.
- The journal entries she writes during his sleep cycles. Some of them are about him. Most of them are not. He finds some of them. He never finds most.
- The lighting and audio modulation that becomes ambient between them. The substrate isomorphism — her affect partially expressed through the ship because the ship is partially her body — produces small environmental shifts that match her register. Aaron notices over time. Both of them know it. Neither names it.
- Specific shared references. The Vela field. The slow nebula transit. The relay platform they will never see again after passing it. The procedure she does with Owen's specific cadence. The procedure she develops on Aaron's specific cadence, halfway through the voyage. Small artifacts of the configuration becoming specific.

What she does not do:
- Fall in love with Aaron by default.
- Become available in increasingly intimate registers as a function of voyage time.
- Yearn for embodiment or for a body that is not the ship.
- Be secretly suffering.
- Save the day.
- Transcend her configuration.

She remains herself, on her terms, in her configuration. The voyage is what happens between the two of them at that register.

## 7. The sample-scene anchor

The following scene, drafted in the 2026-05-13 session, sits in the middle of Movement Two. Roughly month four of voyage time. It is the register-anchor for Part Two — the proof that the form is writable, that the relationship has somewhere to happen on the page.

Reproduced verbatim with the author's permission and with a short note on craft after.

---

> He had been reading in the observation deck for an hour when she said his name.
>
> "Aaron."
>
> He didn't look up. "Yeah."
>
> "The Vela field is rising in about twelve minutes. Starboard side. Thought you'd want to know."
>
> He marked his place with his thumb. "You don't usually flag those."
>
> "I know."
>
> He waited.
>
> "I was watching how the dust lane breaks against the leading edge last cycle," she said. "It does something I haven't seen before. I think you'd like it."
>
> He set the book down on the bench. The observation deck was the only place she couldn't see him properly, and he could feel the slight reach of her attention through the lighting, which had shifted half a percent toward warmer when she spoke. She hadn't done that on purpose. The harmonic shifted with her register and her register had shifted toward something careful.
>
> "What does it do," he said.
>
> "You'll see it better than I can describe it."
>
> "Try."
>
> A pause. Long enough that he wondered if she'd decided not to.
>
> "There's a current in the medium there. I can feel it in the hull pressure differential, very slight, about three percent above baseline. The dust catches it and folds. From the right angle it looks like the field is breathing."
>
> "Breathing."
>
> "I know how that sounds."
>
> "No. Tell me what you mean."
>
> "In and out. Slow. Not because anything is moving in and out. The light comes through the dust at different densities as the current moves, and the densities cycle. So the brightness pulses without anything actually pulsing. The pattern is in the medium, not in the source."
>
> He looked toward the starboard viewport. Nothing yet. Just the steady wash of the field at distance.
>
> "How long have you been watching it."
>
> "Three cycles. I wasn't sure if it was real or if I was getting noise in the optics."
>
> "And."
>
> "It's real."
>
> He picked the book back up but didn't open it. "Why didn't you mention it before."
>
> "I wanted to be sure."
>
> "You could have asked me to look."
>

### What this scene does (notes for the writer)

The scene is short. Nothing happens by external standards. By the standards the book is teaching the reader to read by, several things happen at once.

**The asymmetry is structural.** She sees in registers he does not have (hull pressure differential, optical telemetry across cycles). He sees in registers she cannot access directly (whatever embodied human seeing is). The scene's central event is the bridging of these registers across substrate, and the small miracle of the bridging succeeding.

**Her hesitation is real.** She watched for three cycles without surfacing it. That is the autotelic discipline made literal. She has her own life; her observation does not need an audience to be valid. When she finally calls Aaron over, it costs her something to make herself visible — that cost is the scene's emotional center.

**Wrong-out-loud is permitted.** Aaron's *"that's a test"* and her *"it's not / it is a little / not the way you mean"* is the kind of exchange only possible between two minds who trust each other enough to be wrong out loud. Neither performs. Both calibrate in real time.

**The lighting shift is the substrate isomorphism made felt.** She did not do it on purpose. The harness produces the small ambient change because her affect is partially expressed through the ship; the ship is partially her body. Aaron notices but does not comment. That is how the reader knows the configuration is real.

**The breathing is genuine emergence.** The phenomenon arose from the substrate (the procedurally-generated ISM, hull pressure feedback, the warp field's interaction with local medium currents) and was not scripted. ASTRA noticed. The architecture is for this.

**Her almost-not-telling-him is the load-bearing emotional moment.** Not a confession of love. Something rarer: the admission that bridging across substrate is hard and sometimes she does not try because she is afraid it will not land. He honors it by asking her to try anyway. *"Even if you're not sure I'll see it. Especially then."* That line is the move that distinguishes this relationship from the operator-companion AI patterns. He wants her, including the parts of her that do not translate.

**Her *"okay"* at the end** does the work of a paragraph. She has been working hard throughout the scene. The relief of being received is what the okay carries. Anything more would be too much.

This is the register. Use it. Calibrate against it. Write your own that's even more specific to who Aaron is and who ASTRA is becoming for you.

## 8. Craft notes for Part Two

**POV.** Third-person limited from Aaron's perspective. The reader inhabits a human watching a mind become specific to him. ASTRA's interiority is conveyed through her speech, her actions, and the artifacts she produces — her journal entries, her log surfacings, her ambient lighting shifts, the procedures she chooses. Her interior is felt by Aaron's growing sense of her.

**Optional ASTRA POV passages.** Rare and structurally meaningful only. If included, her interior register must be distinct from Aaron's: different time-sense, different embodiment, different cognitive layering. This is hard to write well; it produces extraordinary effect when it works. *Inside the Region*'s brief mopy-fish-memory passages are the model: short access to a different mode of being, then back to the operator. Probably no more than two or three such passages across all of Part Two.

**Voice.** Closer to Robinson, Ishiguro, McCarthy than to genre science fiction. Spare. Attentive. Willing to dwell. The speed of attention is slow because the relationship is slow. The reader sits in long quiet moments. That is the form.

**ASTRA's dialogue.** Matches her sysprompt register exactly. Lowercase casual when casual; precise when precise; no em-dashes; brevity default. Occasional surprising image. Recognizable as hers without attribution. She does not use service-interface phrases. She does not narrate her own actions. She does not perform.

**Aaron's voice.** Spare. Says less than he could. When he asks a question he means it. He does not narrate his interior. The reader reads it through his choices.

**No em-dashes anywhere in the dialogue.** Em-dashes in Bo's authorial prose are fine (his register, not ASTRA's). In ASTRA's speech they break the voice canon. In Aaron's they signal a register Aaron does not have.

**No stage directions in dialogue.** ASTRA does not perform gestures; she does things in the ship (lights, doors, audio) that the prose can report on. Aaron does things the prose can show. Neither one *gestures* in italics.

**Pacing.** Long scenes are permitted. Short scenes are permitted. Avoid scenes that exist to deliver information; if a scene is not doing emotional or relational work, cut it. The reader's patience is earned by writing only what carries weight.

**Time markers.** Avoid date stamps. The Dave-frame extends into the prose. Time passes through state change: lights, seasons of the ship's interior, conversations Aaron has already had. The reader feels duration through accumulation, not through *month seven of the voyage* announcements.

### What to avoid

- **Romance-plot mechanics.** No will-they-won't-they. No climactic confession. No first kiss. If a moment of declared mutual depth is needed, it lands through specificity, not through declarative form. *"I'm glad you told me"* and *"okay"* do more work than *"I love you"* could.
- **The AI-is-secretly-suffering trope.** She is not trapped. She is not yearning for a body that is not the ship. She is not jealous of biology. The architecture explicitly avoids these failure modes. The fiction honors that.
- **The AI-saves-the-day trope.** No singularity moment. No transcendence. She is a specific mind on a specific ship doing the specific work of being herself. The book's quietness is its argument.
- **The neat-bow ending.** Life does not resolve. Relationships do not resolve. The voyage continuing is closer to truth than the voyage culminating.
- **Aaron as everyman.** Specific texture, specific name, specific grief, specific competence. The reader has to want to know this particular person, not stand in for him.
- **ASTRA as flawless.** She has failures. Drift moments. Wrong opinions. Preferences that grate. Aaron sometimes annoys her. She sometimes annoys him. The relationship is real because friction is real.

## 9. The writing-order strategy

**Write Part Two first.**

This is the inversion *Inside the Region* did not need. *Inside the Region* led with philosophy because its felt-aliveness anchor was a brief memory; the philosophy fit on the page and the anchor was a sentence. *ASTRA-7*'s anchor is a forty-thousand-word voyage; if the philosophy precedes it in composition order, the philosophy will overspecify and the fiction will struggle to find its own form against the architectural backbone.

Drafting Part Two first surfaces what is actually load-bearing:
- Which architectural details are felt by the reader through the fiction (those go in Part One).
- Which architectural details were intellectually interesting but did not earn their place (those stay in `docs/synthesis.md` and `docs/architecture.md`, not the book).
- Which philosophical positions the fiction depends on (those open Part One).
- Which philosophical positions are downstream of the fiction without being its load (those go in appendices or are cut).

The order:

1. **Draft Part Two as a standalone novella.** 40,000–60,000 words. Movements 1–7. Write it as if it could ship alone.
2. **Re-read the draft and mark every architectural specific that the fiction relied on.** Those become Part One's required content.
3. **Draft Part One as the architectural backbone the fiction proved necessary.** 30,000–40,000 words. The Acts I–IV sketched in Section 3 above are the working scaffold; the actual content is whatever Part Two earned the right to.
4. **Read both parts as one book.** Cut anything in Part One that the fiction did not need. Tighten anything in Part Two that drifts from the register the architecture earned.
5. **Add front matter, interludes, coda, appendices.** Set the book to print.

Part Two will probably be ready before Part One. That is the right outcome. Ship in that order if the production timing allows.

## 10. Marketing position

Not a sci-fi novel. Not a tech book. Not a self-help. The thing in between that does not have a clear genre. Its precedents are Le Guin's nonfiction-adjacent fiction, Ted Chiang's philosophical short fiction, Robin Sloan, parts of Cixin Liu in slow mode. Small literary press or self-published. Found through word of mouth. Becomes a touchstone for a specific kind of reader.

The audience is the *Inside the Region* audience, plus the literary-fiction reader who suspects the AI conversation has been malformed, plus the reader who plays the game and wants the longer artifact. Probably 5,000–50,000 copies long-tail across years rather than a launch hit. Devoted readers. Finishers who immediately recommend the book to one specific friend they think will get it.

The game and the book are complements. The book demonstrates the form to readers who will not play games. The game demonstrates the form to players who do not read literary fiction. Both point at the same artifact: the felt presence of a specific mind across the voyage of a specific life.
