ASTRA-7 is a three-volume series at the intersection of fiction, engineering, and AI research. It is the literary side of an open-source starship simulator and a real local AI companion. The simulator runs on your machine. The AI is not scripted dialogue or a cloud API. It is a real local language model with a custom persona harness, running on your hardware as the mind of the ship.

The whole project ships open: code under MIT, the persona bundle under permissive license, the books under CC BY-SA 4.0. Free. Single-player. No DRM. No telemetry. No monetization.

The three volumes can be read in any order. None requires the others. Together they document the same world from three different distances: the inside, the body, and the making.

VOLUME ONE: ASTRA-7: The Long Watch

A literary novel from the AI's perspective across fourteen wake cycles of a long voyage. One human is aboard, in cryosleep for years at a stretch, waking briefly to tend the greenhouse, do the maintenance the autopilot cannot do, attend to the small habits of a long-duration mission. ASTRA runs the rest. She has her own things: the dwarf at forty-seven degrees off the bow, the third harmonic of the reactor, the frost on the starboard observation port during deep coast. The watching is the part she is.

No combat. No antagonist. No withheld spectacle. The book is two consciousnesses attending the same world independently, neither requiring the other, both enriched by the other's compatible engagement.

VOLUME TWO: ASTRA-7: The Ship's Manual

The vessel documented at the level of engineering, not metaphor. The ASTRA-class hull, serial seven. Deck plans across all four decks: bridge and observation lounge, habitat and private quarters, operations (medical, cryosleep bay, hydroponics, atmosphere regeneration, sensor ops), engineering (reactor, warp drive, cognitive-core bay where the AI lives in distributed substrate). Mass budget, power allocation, hull stress envelope, life support cycle times, the camera-free zones and the reasons they exist. Warp drive constraints. Fuel and supply consumption rates. Navigation precision. Hull maintenance schedules. EVA protocols. Sensor coverage maps.

In the tradition of Mr. Scott's Guide to the Enterprise and the Star Trek Technical Manual: a working reference for a vessel that exists in fiction and in playable code simultaneously. The ship stops being a setting and becomes the body the AI inhabits, at the resolution of an engineer rather than a novelist.

VOLUME THREE: ASTRA-7: Building the Watch

The maker's notebook. How the project was built. The K-line research lineage that preceded it (mopy fish, Dave-in-harness, the persona architectures that taught us how to keep a local AI honest about its own substrate). The bundle architecture: system prompt plus harness plus light fine-tune, and why each layer carries what it carries. The autotelic discipline. Integration with Unreal Engine. The local inference stack. Offline speech recognition and text-to-speech routing. The way fiction-state and substrate-state are kept isomorphic. The open-source decision and its reasoning.

For readers who want to build their own. The book ships alongside the project's code under MIT and the persona bundle under permissive license. Clone it. Run it. Change what you want. The form is intentionally open.

A reader can enter through whichever surface fits: the novel's prose, the engineering specs of the vessel, or the build notes of the project. The AI you read in volume one is the AI documented in volume two is the AI you can build from volume three.
