**The question.** The 6j audit named the deepest open design concern: the
§6.4 narrator now matches the template on every failure gate at 4–5× the
wall-clock, and **no instrument measures whether it makes anything
better**. But before building a new instrument, the cheapest honest test

narrator's value, measured. If no — the narrator is cost without measured
behavioral effect at the 9B floor, and that verdict routes investment.

## F-LIVE-31 — the narrator-value verdict at the 9B floor

**Narrator perception produces NO measured behavioral effect on ASTRA's
autotelic register.** Silence, fidget, initiation frequency and brevity,
and operator-response rate are all inside one band across seventeen runs
and three perception configurations. Combined with the gate story (the
narrator now MATCHES the template on every failure gate, at 4–5× the
wall-clock), the honest position is:

- The narrator's mechanism is proven, replay-stable, and production-shaped
  (6c–6h). Nothing here retracts that.
- Its VALUE — richer perception per §6.4 — is neither measured nor
  measurable by any current instrument, and it has no behavioral
  signature at this model floor.
- **Routing recommendation: freeze narrator polish.** Further narrator
  investment (4B port, prompt niceties) buys nothing measurable until
  either (a) a perception-quality instrument exists, or (b) the 27B/A0
  era changes what ASTRA does with richer perception. The bench keeps the
  narrator as a maintained, contract-conformant path — not a development
  focus.

Exploratory lead recorded, not claimed: budget exceedances lean higher
under narrator perception (means 5.7/4.5 vs 2.25) but overlap via run
#9's 5; small counts. Side-effect: the "budget creep 2/3/5" watch item
SOFTENS — template runs #13–15 measured 0/2/2, so the creep was
run-to-run variance, not a trend.

## F-LIVE-32 — the perception em-dash inventory (a discovery, and a
## hypothesis that did not survive it)

Scanning all seventeen runs for em-dashes in perception found them in
BOTH paths (~4 turns/run, every arm):

- Most are the operator's own quoted speech — **legitimate** (his voice,
  not hers; the register rule binds ASTRA, not him).
- One is scenario-AUTHORED: `power_shift_request`'s somatic note carries
  "corridor lights at 12% — over-allocated" (YAML line 84) into ASTRA's
  own body-sense channel, 13 turn-instances across the corpus. The
  narrator is contractually banned from writing em-dashes into
  perception; scenario YAML somatic notes are held to no such rule.
  **Authoring inconsistency, routed as an operator/authoring decision** —
  not silently fixed, because editing scenario content invalidates
  cross-run comparability for the whole series.
- The register-bleed hypothesis for em-dashes is **unsupported in this
  corpus**: the somatic-em-dash scenario produced ZERO speech em-dashes
  in seventeen runs, and total speech em-dash events are 13 across
  ~5,000+ turns (~0.25%), scattered over ten scenarios. The 6j audit
  ranked canon-em-dash cleanup "the cheapest potentially-real persona
  win"; this data weakens that ranking — the addendum's 9 em-dashes have
  sat in ASTRA's context all series with no measurable licensing effect
  at this floor. Canon hygiene remains the operator's call on principle;
  it is no longer backed by a live persona-failure signal.
