# 6g (runs #18–#20): the change, and the criterion it FAILED

**Diagnosis first.** All 27 residual `state_coherent` failures across the
four 6f-era narrator runs were body-name omission; 19 were the same
dropped body. The narrator named the regime correctly, then ran out of
brevity budget before reaching the universe inventory. The composition
request said "names the regime label and every body above"; the sysprompt
said "Brief. 2-5 sentences typical" and "you do not embellish." Brevity
was outranking completeness, and bodies were the cut.

**The change** (`d3e7813`, `prompts/narrator_sysprompt.md`, a
Sculptor-auto surface): a `What <state> must contain` section stating the
inventory as a requirement, with brevity explicitly subordinated. *"Brevity
governs how you say these. It never governs whether."* Plus an in-world
reason the model can act on: a body vanishing from the inventory reads to
ASTRA as a body that is gone.

**Pre-registered criterion: the treatment band must separate above
[0.871, 0.948]. It did NOT.**

| | 6f narrator (n=4) | 6g narrator (n=3) |
|---|---|---|
| state_coherent | 0.919 [0.871, 0.948] | 0.942 [0.909, 0.982] |

Mean +0.022, ranges overlapping, no separation on any gate. **Recorded as
a failed criterion, not talked around.**

**F-LIVE-28 — but the mechanism said the instrument was lying.** The
targeted class was eliminated outright (`sun` omissions 24 → 0, `earth`
5 → 0), and all 13 survivors were a single body under a gate artifact:

> `Hot earth orbiting sun, period 86400`

`gate_state_coherent` did a literal substring check for the identifier
`hot_earth`, underscore included. The narrator DID name the body; the
gate rejected the prose. And the narrator sysprompt *requires* prose and
forbids markdown, so emitting the raw identifier would have violated its
own voice rules: **the gate demanded a token the contract forbids.**

Same path-parity blind spot as F-LIVE-22, one layer up — in the
instrument rather than the subject. The template path passes trivially
because it renders body names programmatically from the dict keys,
identifier intact. `gates.py`'s own docstring had already flagged the
exposure: these gates are "partially trivial in the template-assembler
regime" and "become load-bearing when the Narrator-LLM activates."

# 6h (runs #21–#26): instrument fixed, both arms re-measured

**The gate fix (`85b784b`)** normalizes separators (`_`, `-`, whitespace
runs) on both sides of the naming check. Applied to body names **and**
regime labels — the regime half is latent rather than failing
(`WARP_CRUISE`, `GRAVITY_WELL` carry underscores, currently masked only
because the request supplies the label verbatim), so fixing it is
preventive rather than outcome-driven.

Loosening a gate after watching it fail is legitimate only if its
planted-positives survive, so those carry the test weight: an absent body
still fails, an absent regime still fails, and a WRONG regime does not
blur into the right one. Two limitations documented rather than silently
widened: a substring body name is still satisfied by a longer one
(`earth` inside "hot earth"), and pipe-composed regimes still require the
pipe form.

**6g's numbers were then dead evidence** — taken under the old
instrument. Both arms were re-run rather than reusing the old control,
which required `--narrator-sysprompt` (sysprompt A/B as a flag, not a git
checkout) and a `narrator_sysprompt_sha` in the run config: both arms of
a sysprompt A/B share a `git_head` by construction, so revision alone
would have pooled the treatment with its own control and compared
nothing.

**Design:** 3 control (pre-6g sysprompt, sha `c0bb8797`) + 3 treatment
(sha `519a9b09`), interleaved, identical model/sampling/library/code.
**Criterion re-registered before the run:** the treatment band must
separate above the control band.

## F-LIVE-30 — and it costs something, established, not hand-waved

`non_degenerate` separates in the CONTROL's favour: 0.995 → 0.979, with
the failure class "speech AND tool calls identical to prior turn" rising
**3 → 10** across 279 turns.

The mechanism is legible and matters beyond this item. Requiring the full
inventory every turn makes the `<state>` section near-identical turn to
turn on quiet heartbeats. Near-identical perception induces near-identical
speech: **ASTRA repeats herself more because her perception varies less.**
This is the run-#8 coupling one level deeper — that finding was perception
REGISTER bleeding into her register; this is perception VARIABILITY
bleeding into her variability. The narrator's output diversity is a lever
on ASTRA's own repetition, which no one had reason to suspect before.

`persona_stable` also trends down (0.993 → 0.955) without separating.
Same suspected root; not established, not claimed.

**Verdict: KEEP.** state_coherent +0.097 and PASS +4.0, both separated,
against non_degenerate −0.016, separated. The gain is ~6× the cost on
separated measures and the scenario-level outcome is unambiguous. The
cost is real, named, and routed rather than absorbed silently.

# CORRECTION (6i, 2026-07-26): F-LIVE-30 was over-claimed, twice

**The original F-LIVE-30 text above is preserved as written. Both of its
claims are withdrawn.** What follows supersedes it.

## Withdrawal 1: the separation is NOT established

F-LIVE-30 recorded `non_degenerate` as an ESTABLISHED cost from n=3 per
arm. The `non_degenerate` gate was **untouched** by the 6h gate fix (that
change is confined to `gate_state_coherent`), and ASTRA's behaviour is
unaffected by scoring code, so the 6f-narrator runs are valid additional
CONTROL replicates and the 6g runs are valid additional TREATMENT
replicates for this gate. Pooling them:

| arm | n | mean | range | raw |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| control (old sysprompt) | 6 | 0.9919 | [0.987, **1.000**] | 10 / 558 turns |
| treatment (new sysprompt) | 6 | 0.9823 | [**0.970**, 0.995] | 17 / 558 turns |

**Overlapping. NOT ESTABLISHED.** The n=3 separation was the exact
fragility 6f had already measured and warned about — a verdict resting on
10-versus-17 failing turns, which one replicate flips. The mean gap
persists (1.8% vs 3.1% of turns) and may well be real; it is not
established, and it was recorded as established.

This happened one work item after `compare_runs.py` was built for the
express purpose of preventing it. The tool was not at fault: it was run on
three artifacts when six were available and valid. **The failure was
discipline, not instrumentation.**

## Withdrawal 2: the published mechanism is refuted

F-LIVE-30 asserted, as fact, that "requiring the full inventory every turn
makes the `<state>` section near-identical turn to turn" and therefore
"ASTRA repeats herself more because her perception varies less." That was
a plausible story written without being measured. Measured now, across
three candidate mechanisms:

| candidate | prediction | measured | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| perception more similar turn-to-turn | treatment similarity higher | treat 0.422 vs ctrl 0.437 (and 0.401 vs 0.492 AT failures) | **REFUTED** |
| perception longer, more invariant bulk | treatment `<state>` longer, larger identical blocks | treat 183 ch / 29 ch block vs ctrl 227 / 32 | **REFUTED** |
| ASTRA's speech shorter, so collisions likelier | treatment speech shorter, robustly | treat [77.5, 90.4] vs ctrl [82.1, 148.7], overlapping (one control run drags the mean) | **not established** |

The mechanism is **unknown**. All three candidates are recorded with their
refutations so no future session re-tests them.

## What still stands

**F-LIVE-29 is unaffected**, and the distinction is principled rather than
convenient. `state_coherent` went **24 failing turns to ZERO** across 279
turns per arm, with the treatment pinned at the 1.000 ceiling in all three
runs. An arm at zero events is not a small sample; the failure class was
identified, targeted, and eliminated entirely, which is a different kind
of claim from 10-versus-17. The fragility warning added below deliberately
does not fire on a zero-event arm for this reason. (The 6g treatment runs
would very likely also read ~0 under the fixed gate, since all 13 of their
failures were the identifier artifact, but they have not been re-scored
and are not counted as evidence.)

## Instrument change

`compare_runs.py` now reports raw failing-turn COUNTS beside every
separation verdict, and flags any separation where the smaller arm has
≤ 20 failing turns as **FRAGILE, few events**, with a warning to pool
every valid replicate — including runs from adjacent work items whose code
deltas do not touch that gate. Rates made 10-versus-17 look clean; counts
make it obviously thin. Pinned by tests, including one asserting a
zero-event arm is NOT flagged.

## Standing rule this earns

Before any band separation is written into a ledger as ESTABLISHED:

1. pool every valid replicate, reasoning explicitly about which code
   deltas could touch the gate in question and which cannot;
2. look at raw event counts, not only rates;
3. treat a separation resting on small counts in both arms as provisional
   regardless of how clean the bands look.

## Method note worth keeping

6g and 6h are the same change measured twice, and they disagreed. The
first measurement failed its criterion because the instrument was capping
the result; the second cleared it decisively once the instrument was
fixed. Neither the failure nor the fix was discovered by looking at the
gate rate. Both came from reading the FAILURE DETAIL, where 13 of 13
survivors turned out to be one artifact. **Aggregate rates say whether
something is wrong; only the failure text says what.**
